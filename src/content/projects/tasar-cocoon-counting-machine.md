India produces all four major kinds of silk, and tasar silk is a source of livelihood for thousands of farmers in states such as Jharkhand, Odisha, Bihar, Chhattisgarh and West Bengal. This project looks at a problem farmers face when they sell: counting and grading cocoons.

The machine is designed to cost around ₹15,000 when built at scale, far less than industrial equipment. It is meant for controlled testing first, with sensor limits calibrated on real tasar cocoons. Large-scale testing with farmers is planned as a later stage.

Planned improvements include machine learning to spot defects, mobile-phone monitoring, solar power, and sensors for moisture and cocoon strength.

The project was a national winner at the CBSE level, was selected to represent at Rashtriya Bal Vaigyanik Pradarshni, and won 3rd rank at the Sahodaya regional level.
